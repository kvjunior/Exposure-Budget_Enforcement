# Scientific scope of the released artifact

This release corresponds to *Coordinated Exposure-Budget Enforcement for Encrypted Data Services with Intermittent Owners*. The finite model represents a declared family of colluding coalitions and a conservative opening relation. Model-level assertions, synthetic run-level outcomes and isolated cryptographic functional checks have different evidentiary roles.

- The controller sees publicly available records/reports and frozen observable views.
- A separately trusted enforcement boundary checks candidate capability changes against actual records and retained capabilities.
- Production fencing checks scope reconstructibility before binding a newly produced record, activating a fresh scope when authority exists or refusing the production.
- The cumulative first-disclosure ledger is irreversible under the model's authorization semantics.
- Whole-run compliance is conditional on accurate closure semantics, independent fresh keys, authorized transition handling and complete mediation.
- The full-horizon experiment is synthetic: three regimes, two budgets, 30 paired seeds per cell, five policies, and four demands/eight planned production events per policy-run.
- Cost figures are abstract model units; real crypto latency is outside the measured simulation.
- Two bounded-oracle experiments and a single-demand independent reference do not constitute a general greedy-optimality theorem.

The current authority for the published experiment is `python/experiments/p3e_workload_eval.py`, its raw data in `results/manifests/`, and the article in `paper/main.tex`. Historical notes remain in `docs/` for auditability.
